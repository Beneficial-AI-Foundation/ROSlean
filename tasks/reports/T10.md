# T10 — Demo: Nav2 launches the Lean node

Branch: `claude/cm-nav2-demo`            Base: `8152520` of `origin/collision-monitor`

Run on a local macOS arm64 machine (18 cores, ROS 2 Jazzy from RoboStack
through pixi, Nav2 and `nav2_collision_monitor` 1.3.12, Gazebo Sim 8.10) on
the orchestrator's instruction; `scripts/cloud_setup.sh` was not run and no
pull request was opened. The machine has a display, but **its screen was
locked for the whole task**.

"Observed" below means seen in a run made here; "inferred" means concluded
from reading or from geometry and not run.

## Done

1. **The variant** (`ros/roslean_collision_monitor/demo/gazebo/`):
   - `cm_nav2_launch.py`: Gazebo, the robot, the bridge,
     `robot_state_publisher`, and Nav2 as `bringup_launch.py` starts it
     with `use_composition:=False`: `nav2_bringup`'s
     `localization_launch.py` unchanged and `navigation_launch.py` with one
     line changed (`package='nav2_collision_monitor'` to
     `package='roslean_collision_monitor'` in the `Node` action). The edit
     is made on the installed file when the launch starts and refuses to
     go on if that line is not found exactly once. No mux, no base
     watchdog, no second monitor. Arguments:
     `monitor_package:=nav2_collision_monitor` (the unedited launch file,
     same parameters), `zones:=approach` (stock Nav2 parameters, upstream's
     node only), `driver:=tour|patrol|none`, `scenario`, `gui`, `rviz`,
     `furniture`.
   - `nav2_monitor.yaml`: the `collision_monitor` section. Against
     `nav2_bringup`'s: `enable_stamped_cmd_vel: true` added, and
     `FootprintApproach` replaced by two fixed polygons, `FootprintStop`
     (x −0.155…0.305, y ±0.155) and `FootprintSlow` (x −0.205…0.705,
     y ±0.205, ratio 0.4), `min_points: 6`, `visualize: True`. Everything
     else is Nav2's (`state_topic`, `base_shift_correction: True`, topics,
     timeouts). The other sections are the twist_mux demo's
     `nav2_params.yaml` (the burger's size and speeds, stamped velocities,
     AMCL's start pose).
   - The margins (the demo's choice, stated as such in the tutorial): the
     footprint is the square ±0.105 m (`robot_radius`); the stop zone
     reaches 0.20 m further ahead and 0.05 m further to the sides and
     behind. Ahead: 4 cm travelled between two scans at 0.2 m/s, a scan
     that old again, 1 cm of noise, and the rest so that the stop comes
     before the patrol script's own guard (0.30 m from the lidar).
     Sideways and behind small on purpose, because a stop is absorbing.
   - Nodes that ran (observed, from the ROS graph during the check):
     `map_server`, `amcl`, `lifecycle_manager_localization`,
     `controller_server` (with `local_costmap`), `smoother_server`,
     `planner_server` (with `global_costmap`), `route_server`,
     `behavior_server`, `velocity_smoother`, `collision_monitor` (the Lean
     executable), `bt_navigator`, `waypoint_follower`, `docking_server`,
     `lifecycle_manager_navigation`; beside them `robot_state_publisher`,
     `ros_gz_bridge`, the patrol or the tour script, the narrator. So the
     whole Nav2 stack, not only the velocity chain.

## Found wrong or changed

- **With the stop zone, Nav2's tour ends with the robot standing for good
  in three runs of six** (two of three with the Lean node, one of three
  with upstream's, same parameters); stock Nav2 with `approach` did not
  stand in its one run. Reproduce:
  `python3 ros/roslean_collision_monitor/demo/gazebo/nav2_observe.py tour --seconds 300 [--monitor nav2_collision_monitor] [--zones approach]`.
  Each time near the kitchen, the second place. Nav2 logs `Failed to make
  progress`, runs spin and back-up, they fail (the monitor zeroes them),
  the goal is aborted and sent again. The measuring script's own count
  found 9 readings inside the stop rectangle at the end of both runs that
  recorded it, so the stops follow the rule; this is the behaviour of a
  `stop` zone, not a defect of either node. After the first two Lean runs
  and the first upstream run it looked like a difference between the
  nodes (1, 1 against 8 places); the two further runs of each showed it is
  not (8 with the Lean node, 2 with upstream's). Three runs each do not
  compare the nodes.
- **Nav2 did not come up on the first start** in a fresh environment
  (above). Not the monitor's doing (inferred from the log: the lifecycle
  manager was waiting on `behavior_server`, and
  `lifecycle_manager_localization` had printed nothing). It did not happen
  again in 30 further starts of the variant. The old tutorial reported the
  same kind of failure for the older Nav2 variant.
- **`docking_server` publishes on `cmd_vel`** beside the monitor (observed
  in the ROS graph): Nav2's `navigation_launch.py` remaps the controller
  and the behaviours to `cmd_vel_nav` but not the docking server, so its
  commands bypass the collision monitor in stock Nav2 as well. Noted in the
  tutorial.
- **Flicker at the edge of the stop zone was seen in 7 of 10 runs** (3 of 4
  with upstream) in the Nav2 variant, against none in three runs behind
  the mux. Not investigated; zones, `min_points` and base shift correction
  all differ.
- The tutorial described the node as it was before T06 in several places
  (sensor pose parameter, "not a lifecycle node", "shows nothing of its
  state", "refuses the parameters Nav2 ships" at three places). Rewritten;
  what remains true (no `approach`) is now the first item of step 9.
- The check's old table (nine runs, step 5) was measured before the node
  read TF. Left in place and marked; three runs with the present node are
  quoted beside it.

## Questions for the user

1. The demo's stop zone traps Nav2 on its tour about every second run.
   Should the documented Nav2 edit stay "a fixed stop polygon", with this
   said (as the tutorial now does), or should the tutorial recommend a
   slowdown zone only for use under a real planner, until `approach`
   exists?
2. The margins (0.20 m ahead, 0.05 m sideways and behind) were chosen for
   the scenario, the forward one partly so that the stop precedes the
   patrol script's guard. Is a demo-specific choice acceptable, or should
   the patrol's guard be made switchable so that the margin can be argued
   from the scan rate alone? (`patrol.py` belongs to the twist_mux demo and
   was not touched.)
3. The two pictures are Gazebo's own captures of the scene. Keep them
   until someone takes screenshots at an unlocked screen, or remove them?
4. `tasks/README.md` says a check that needs the whole of Nav2 inherits
   Nav2's start-up; one start in about thirty failed here. Should the
   launch file wait for the simulated clock before it starts Nav2? (Not
   tried.)

## Friction

- macOS has no `timeout`; background jobs of a non-interactive shell ignore
  SIGINT, so `ros2 launch … &` followed by `kill -INT` hangs. Processes
  were stopped through `check_demo.stop_demo` or by PID.
- The installed demo files are symlinks into the source tree
  (`--symlink-install`), and `cm-check` runs the script from the source
  tree, so nothing under `demo/` could be edited while a series ran.
- Nav2's launch file sets `RCUTILS_LOGGING_BUFFERED_STREAM=1`: the log of a
  running bring-up lags by minutes, which made the first failure look like
  a hang of the logging.
- The locked screen: discovered only by reading the captured image.
- `ROS_DOMAIN_ID` 200–209 lie in macOS's ephemeral port range; no effect
  was seen.
