## Not run, and why

- **Linux, in any form.** This machine is macOS. Every [B] is for the
  macOS arm64 RoboStack binary only. In particular whether the Linux
  binaries fuse the transform's multiply-adds was not examined.
- **`lake build` of the root package, `pixi run build`, `pixi run
  test-quick`, `pixi run test`.** This task changes no Lean source of a
  package, no C++ and no test; the only Lean file added is a stand-alone
  prototype outside every package. `pixi run test` was forbidden by the
  orchestrator (another agent was running the colcon tests, and the tests
  choose their own ROS domains, outside the range given to this task).
  `test-quick` and the rule to run it arrived with the final merge of
  `origin/collision-monitor`; it was not run, for the same two reasons.
- **`tasks/reports/T04.progress`** (PLAN.md, "Watching the tasks", also new
  with the final merge): written once at the end, not step by step.
- **The harness scenarios `offset`, `rotated`, `transform-wait`,
  `no-transform`, `no-odom`** of `scenario.py`: not re-run; SPEC_NAV2.md
  cites UPSTREAM_BEHAVIOUR.md for them and does not mark them [B]. My probes
  cover the same ground with their own cases.
- **The real local costmap** as publisher of the footprint (N30): read from
  source only. The footprint messages in `footprint-sub-topic` are
  published by the probe.
- **Nav2's bring-up itself** (`navigation_launch.py`, composition): read
  from source only. The lifecycle manager binary alone was run against the
  node (`manager`).
- **Not probed**, and listed at the end of the test plan: the interpolation
  of N24 at a zone boundary; a quarter-turn of the base with correction;
  inexact quaternions at a boundary; a backward clock jump; `autostart_node`;
  the component form of upstream's node; `~/toggle` and run-time `enabled`.
- **A proof of the bound on upstream's floating-point error** (10⁻⁶ mm):
  it is an estimate (T7), stated as such.

## Found wrong or changed

In the task files, from what the source and the binary show:

1. **The edit "change `action_type` of Nav2's default `FootprintApproach`
   zone to `stop` or `slowdown`, nothing else" does not work on upstream.**
   `footprint_topic` is read for `approach` only
   (`Polygon::getCommonParameters`); a zone without `points` and with
   another action needs `polygon_sub_topic`, and without it `configure`
   fails: `parameter 'FootprintApproach.polygon_sub_topic' is not
   initialized`, `static points and sub topic both not defined`.
   Reproduce: `run_nav2_probe.sh <prefix> footprint-edit` (both
   `nav2_params.yaml` and the package's example file, `stop` and
   `slowdown`). T04's own brief, T06 item 6 and T07 item 6 assumed the
   edit works. What works: adding `polygon_sub_topic:
   "/local_costmap/published_footprint"` (`footprint-sub-topic`), or giving
   the zone fixed `points`. T06 and T07 are corrected; SPEC_NAV2.md, 8.6,
   has both forms.
2. **"Transform unavailable" is not an event** (T05 item 1, T06 item 4).
   Upstream looks the transform up at every command, so the outcome is a
   field of the command event. Corrected in T05 and T06.
3. **The transform is not planar** (T05 item 2): upstream applies the full
   3D rotation and drops z. Corrected.
4. **"Footprint zones"** (T06 item 5, T07 item 3) are zones fed by
   `polygon_sub_topic`; corrected, with the QoS found (best effort; a
   latched message is not delivered).
5. **T06 "May change"** now includes the package's `package.xml` and the
   non-harness part of its `CMakeLists.txt`, which the new dependencies
   need. PLAN.md's ownership table does not list them for T06; the
   orchestrator may want to adjust it.
6. **T07** is told that the lookups wait in node-clock time, that every
   command under base shift correction needs a newer odom sample, and
   which differences a scenario can see.

In upstream, flagged in SPEC_NAV2.md, section 2 (each with its probe case):

- A stop zone fed by a topic does nothing until its first message
  (`polygon-topic`, `footprint-sub-topic`).
- A footprint zone fed from a topic in the odom frame stays where it was
  published: the base moved 1 m with no new message, and points 0.1 m ahead
  of it passed while points 1.1 m behind it stopped (`footprint-sub-topic`).
- A node shut down from the active state keeps forwarding commands
  (`lifecycle`, last step).
- Deactivate and activate restarts the `stop_pub_timeout` period and
  re-reports the state (`lifecycle`).
- A polygon message with a NaN coordinate is accepted and the zone no longer
  contains a point inside the box (`polygon-topic`).
- Without base shift correction the newest transform is used whatever its
  age, including one stamped after the scan and the clock (`tf-latest`).
- A sensor pitched by a quarter turn puts a point 3 m away on the base's
  origin (`tf-pitch-circle`).
- `~/toggle` survives cleanup (`lifecycle`, service list).

In this repository's documents: nothing was changed outside the task's
files. SPEC.md's D11 says upstream "by default" corrects for motion and a
failed lookup stops; both confirmed. SPEC.md, "Excluded features", row
"Footprint subscription: only with `approach`" is right and is the reason
for finding 1.

One design point departs from the wording of my brief. It asks for events
"so that every failure case stops the robot". Two cases do not stop,
because upstream does not and tasks/README.md says to keep upstream's
behaviour where the decision is the user's: a missing transform with
`source_timeout: 0`, and a topic zone that has no shape yet. Both are
stated as such in the properties ("No transform" with its hypotheses, "No
shape") and are questions 1 and 4.

## Questions for the user

In full in SPEC_NAV2.md, section 13.

1. A stop zone fed by a topic that has no shape yet takes no action, as
   upstream. Make it stop instead?
2. A footprint zone that is left behind when its publisher stops: accept,
   give shapes in another frame a maximum age, or document only the fixed
   polygon?
3. Transform in C++ (A) or in Lean (B)? Recommended: B. Measured cost: 1.2
   to 1.9 µs per reading and command in exact arithmetic, against 41 ns per
   reading and scan today.
4. `source_timeout: 0` also disables the stop for a missing transform, as
   upstream. Keep?
5. The wait for a transform inside the command callback is reproduced as
   upstream does it. Agreed?
6. After a shutdown from the active state the Lean node is silent where
   upstream keeps forwarding (ND4). Agreed?
7. Is a stand-alone process acceptable if the node cannot be loaded into
   Nav2's component container (ND10)? `bringup_launch.py` uses composition
   by default.
8. Which form of the Nav2 parameter edit to document: the footprint as a
   fixed polygon (recommended), the two-line change of Nav2's zone, or
   both?

## Friction

- The worktree guard of the session refused any shell command that mixed
  `git` with pipes, redirections or heredocs, also for clones outside the
  repository. Each such step had to be split or moved into a script file.
- `nav2_lifecycle_manager` failed its bring-up (`get_state service client:
  async_send_request failed`) while the probe polled the same `get_state`
  service in a loop; with a single query after eight seconds it succeeded.
  Cause not investigated; a harness that watches a managed node should not
  hammer its lifecycle services.
- Under simulated time a failed lookup blocks the node until the clock
  moves, so a probe that forgets to advance the clock sees "no answer" and
  cannot tell a stop from a hang. Two probe cases had to be redesigned
  because their first version could not distinguish two outcomes (above).
- Lean: a helper named `ofBits` at the root was shadowed by `Xf.ofBits`
  inside `Xf.apply`, with an error about `UInt64 → … → Xf` that did not say
  so. I found no left shift on `Int` and used a cast of `1 <<< n` on `Nat`
  in place of `(2 : Int) ^ n` (one earlier timing run, with `^`, was
  about 20 % slower). The fixed-point variant still costs 0.45 µs per point
  on `Int`; a fast version would need `Int64` with overflow proofs.
- `lean -c` insists that the file is under the directory of the toolchain
  file; the prototype is copied to a temporary directory to be compiled.
- The one outlier in the timing (177 µs) cost a rerun series and is still
  unexplained.
